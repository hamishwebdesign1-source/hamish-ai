1. Executive Summary

1.1 Business Problem

Hamish AI is an Edinburgh-based consultancy that designs and delivers AI-powered automation for small businesses. Prior to this platform, the business ran its own client operations — request handling, task tracking, lead generation, and billing — through informal, manual channels: email, phone calls and memory, with no central system of record. As the client base has grown, this created a real operational risk: requests could be lost or delayed, lead follow-up depended on the operator remembering to chase, invoices were raised and chased manually, and clients had no way to check progress without contacting the operator directly. The business problem is therefore twofold: (a) the operational risk of running a growing client-service business on ad hoc tooling, and (b) a credibility gap — a consultancy that sells AI-driven operational automation to its clients was not, itself, running on one.

1.2 Current State

Client requests arrived through unstructured channels with no formal lifecycle, lead prospecting was tracked in a spreadsheet with no automated reminder, invoicing (where it existed at all) was raised manually with no chase mechanism, and website health for client sites was checked reactively — typically only once a client reported a problem. Section 5 (Current State Analysis) documents this in full, mapped against the same process structure used for the target operating model.

1.3 Future State

The platform now in operation implements a seven-process operating model — Lead Generation, Client Onboarding, Request Handling & Fulfillment, Client Support, Site Monitoring, Billing, and a Daily Overview reporting layer — each supported by purpose-built automation and, in five specific decision points, by AI. An internal admin console gives the operator a single place to manage clients, requests, tasks, leads and invoices; a client-facing self-service portal gives clients visibility of their own requests, tasks, website health and billing without needing to contact the operator. The complete flow is captured in the HamishAI Business Process Model (the reference diagram at /admin/process) and every artefact in this pack is traceable back to it.

1.4 Expected Benefits

  • Reduced administrative overhead per client through automated triage, task creation, calendar scheduling and client notification.
  • Faster, more consistent client response times, including automatic resolution of routine, maintenance-covered requests.
  • Improved cash flow predictability through automatic invoice status reconciliation and one-click overdue reminders.
  • A repeatable new-business pipeline through scheduled lead research, AI-drafted outreach and automatic follow-up flagging.
  • Earlier detection of client website issues, ahead of the client noticing and reporting them.
  • A scalable operating model that supports growth in client volume without a proportional increase in operator time.

1.5 Success Measures

Success is measured through the KPI set defined in Section 26 (Process Metrics), spanning auto-resolution rate, time-to-first-response, lead conversion and follow-up compliance, on-time invoice payment rate, site-issue detection rate and portal adoption. These metrics are drawn directly from the underlying data model documented in Section 15 (Data Requirements), so they can be reported without any additional instrumentation.

2. Business Objectives

2.1 Strategic Objectives

  • Scale client-service capacity without a proportional increase in operator headcount or hours.
  • Establish Hamish AI's own operations as a live, working reference case for the AI-automation services it sells to clients.
  • Improve cash-flow predictability through automated billing status tracking and payment reminders.
  • Build a repeatable, low-effort client-acquisition pipeline (research → outreach → follow-up → onboarding).

  • Reduced incidence of late payment through automatic reminders on overdue invoices.
  • Consistent, auditable client communication regardless of which channel a client used to make contact.

3. Scope

3.1 In Scope

  • Client record management, including maintenance plan and package tracking (Process 2 — Client Onboarding).
  • Multi-channel request capture (client portal and monitored email inbox) and AI-assisted triage (Process 3).
  • Automatic reply for high-confidence, maintenance-covered requests; clarification flagging for incomplete requests (Process 3).
  • Task creation, calendar scheduling and client notification on completion (Process 3).
  • Client self-service support via an AI-answered knowledge base (Process 4).
  • Scheduled website health monitoring and alerting (Process 5).
  • Invoice creation, delivery, payment reconciliation and overdue reminders via a real payment processor (Process 6).
  • Weekly automated lead research, AI-drafted outreach and stale-lead follow-up flagging (Process 1).
  • A consolidated daily Overview dashboard for the operator (Process 7).
  • A visual, maintained Business Process Model and this accompanying documentation pack.
  • Passwordless (magic-link) access to the admin console as an alternative to the shared password.

3.2 Out of Scope

  • Recurring or subscription-based invoicing for maintenance-plan clients (identified as a Phase 2 candidate — see Section 32, Recommendations).
  • Automatic, unattended card-on-file payment collection (all payment collection is via a client-initiated hosted payment page).
  • Multi-operator or role-based access control within the admin console (single-operator model only).
  • Native mobile applications (the portal and admin console are responsive web applications only).
  • CRM-grade marketing automation beyond a single AI-drafted outreach message and one follow-up flag.
  • Multi-currency billing (GBP only).
  • Formally contracted SLA guarantees to clients.

3.3 Assumptions

  • ASM-01: Hamish remains the sole system operator for the documented scope of this platform.
  • ASM-02: All clients are UK-registered small businesses invoiced in GBP.
  • ASM-03: Clients are willing and able to use a web-based self-service portal; no dedicated client-side application support is required.
  • ASM-04: The payment processor account will be promoted from test/sandbox mode to live mode before any real client is charged.
  • ASM-05: The Google Cloud OAuth application will either be reconnected manually every 7 days or be moved out of Testing publishing status.
  • ASM-06: Client, request and lead volumes remain within the practical review capacity of a single operator for the documented scope; no formal capacity ceiling is defined in this release.

(Full assumptions log with cross-references: Section 20.)

3.4 Constraints

  • The hosting platform's entry-level plan restricts scheduled (cron) jobs to a once-daily frequency, so several processes are necessarily batch/daily rather than real-time (inbound email ingestion, site-health checks, the weekly digest).
  • The platform depends on the continuous availability of five external SaaS providers (AI, data/auth, payments, email delivery, calendar/inbox).
  • Single-operator bandwidth constrains the throughput of Process 3 (Request Handling) regardless of the degree of automation applied to triage.

3.5 Dependencies

Full dependency log with ownership: Section 21. In summary, the platform depends on: the AI provider (Anthropic), the data and authentication platform (Supabase), the payment processor (Stripe), the email delivery provider (Resend), the calendar and inbox provider (Google Workspace APIs), and the hosting/scheduling platform (Vercel), plus DNS/domain resolution for hamishai.org.

5. Current State Analysis (As-Is)

5.1 Existing Process

Before this platform, client requests were received by phone, email or in person with no central log. The operator judged each request individually and replied ad hoc, with no formal record of what had been promised or when. Prospective new business (leads) were tracked in a spreadsheet, researched irregularly, and had no reminder mechanism for re-engagement. Invoicing, where it existed, was raised manually with no automated chase for late payment. Client website health was checked reactively, generally only once a client reported that something appeared broken. There was no client-facing channel through which a client could check progress without contacting the operator directly.

6. Future State Analysis (To-Be)

6.1 New Process

A single integrated platform now implements the seven-process operating model set out in the HamishAI Business Process Model: Lead Generation, Client Onboarding, Request Handling & Fulfillment, Client Support, Site Monitoring, Billing, and Daily Overview reporting. Every request, regardless of channel, is logged centrally and triaged automatically. High-confidence, maintenance-covered requests are answered without operator involvement; incomplete requests are flagged with specific clarifying questions rather than left for manual review. Genuine work becomes a tracked task with a calendar entry, and the client is notified automatically when it is done.

6.2 AI Automation

Five specific decisions are AI-assisted rather than manual: request triage classification, auto-send eligibility, missing-information detection, lead-to-case-study matching for outreach, and knowledge-base question answering. Full detail, including inputs, logic, outputs, confidence handling and human override, is provided in Section 18 (AI Decision Points).

6.3 Improved Customer Journey

A prospective business is researched and contacted automatically, follows up if unanswered, and — once onboarded — gains a self-service portal covering request submission and history, task status, website health, billing, and an instant AI-answered question channel. The client's entire relationship with Hamish AI is visible in one place, without needing to contact the operator for a status update.

6.4 Operational Improvements and Expected Efficiencies

  • Elimination of manual first-pass triage for every incoming request.
  • Automatic task and calendar creation removes a manual scheduling step for every piece of real work.
  • Automatic invoice status reconciliation removes manual payment-confirmation chasing.
  • One-click overdue reminders replace ad hoc, easily-forgotten chase emails.
  • Weekly automated lead research removes a recurring manual prospecting task.
  • A single Overview dashboard replaces the need to check four separate areas of the console for outstanding work.

Full efficiency and benefit quantification: Section 26 (Process Metrics) and Section 27 (Benefits Realisation).

9. Functional Requirements

Each functional requirement implements one or more business requirements above. Inputs, outputs, business rules and exception handling are stated per function; detailed validations are covered where they materially affect behaviour.

ID

Function

Inputs

Outputs

Business Rules / Exceptions

FR-01

Log and triage an incoming request (implements BR-01, BR-02)

Raw request text; source channel (portal form or monitored inbox message); client identity

Request record with category, complexity, priority and maintenance-coverage determination

A monitored-inbox message is matched to a client by sender email; unmatched senders are not triaged. A message already labelled as processed is never re-triaged (RULE-10).

FR-02

Determine auto-send or clarification flag (implements BR-03, BR-04)

Triage output (category, complexity, coverage determination)

Either an auto-sent client reply, or an awaiting_info status with explicit missing-information questions

Auto-send requires high confidence AND maintenance coverage (RULE-01); any other outcome defaults to awaiting_info or task creation, never a silent drop.

FR-03

Create a task from a triaged request (implements BR-05)

Triaged request classified as requiring real work

Task record linked to the source request, with a title, description and due date

Due date is derived from priority per RULE-03. A request may generate at most one active task.

FR-04

Synchronise a task to the operator's calendar (implements BR-05)

New task record

A calendar event with a due date, title referencing the client, and a link back to the request

If calendar sync is not configured (no valid credential), the task is still created; only the calendar step is skipped.

FR-05

Notify the client on task completion (implements BR-06)

Task status change to done

Client email confirming completion

Only a transition to done triggers an email; todo and in_progress transitions are silent, as the client has no action to take on them.

FR-06

Run scheduled lead research (implements BR-07)

Defined geographic scope (Central Belt of Scotland) and category criteria

New prospect records: business name, category, neighbourhood, website, contact email (where found), initial score and signal

Duplicate businesses already present are not re-added; a new prospect defaults to needs_verification status.

FR-07

Draft a personalised outreach email (implements BR-08)

Prospect record (category, business name, signal)

Draft subject and body, opened ready to send in the operator's email client

The prospect's category is matched against a maintained case-study list (Section 18, Decision 4); no match is forced if none is a confident fit.

FR-08

Flag a stale contacted lead for follow-up (implements BR-09)

Prospect status and last-contacted timestamp

A visible needs-follow-up flag on the lead

Applies only where status = contacted and more than 5 days have elapsed since contacted_at (RULE-04).

FR-09

Create and send a client invoice (implements BR-10)

Client selection, amount, description (entered by operator)

A payment-processor invoice, a stored invoice record, and a client email containing the hosted payment link

An invoice cannot be created for a client without a verified email on file (RULE-07). Due date defaults to 14 days from creation (RULE-06).

FR-10

Reconcile invoice status via payment webhook (implements BR-11)

Signed webhook event from the payment processor

Updated invoice status (paid / void / uncollectible / payment failed) and, where paid, a paid timestamp

The webhook signature must verify before any data is trusted or written; unverifiable events are rejected outright.

FR-11

Send an overdue-invoice reminder (implements BR-12)

Operator-initiated action on an invoice flagged overdue

A reminder email to the client with the payment link, and a recorded reminder-sent timestamp

Only invoices with status = open and a due date in the past may be reminded (RULE-05); the action is one-click but never automatic, so the operator can first confirm the client hasn't already paid out-of-band.

FR-12

Run a daily site-health check and alert on issues (implements BR-13, BR-14)

Each client's registered website URL

A stored check result (uptime, SSL validity, broken links, AI summary) and, if unhealthy, an alert email

Only clients with a website URL on file are checked; a healthy result updates the portal summary silently.

FR-13

Present a client's own data in the self-service portal (implements BR-15)

Authenticated client session

Request history, task status, website-health summary and invoice/billing status, scoped to that client only

Only fields appropriate for client visibility are ever returned; internal-only fields (see Section 14, RULE-08) are excluded at the query level, not merely hidden in the interface.

FR-14

Answer a client question from the knowledge base (implements BR-16)

Client's typed question

A drafted answer sourced only from matched knowledge-base entries

If no relevant knowledge-base entry exists, the answer indicates this rather than fabricating a response.

FR-15

Present the consolidated Overview dashboard (implements BR-17)

Current state of invoices, requests, leads and site checks across all clients

Counts and itemised lists of overdue invoices, stuck requests, stale leads and site issues, or confirmation that none exist

Each list links directly to the underlying record so the operator can act immediately.

FR-16

Provide passwordless admin sign-in (implements BR-19)

Operator-submitted email address

A one-time sign-in link emailed to that address; on click, an authenticated admin session

The link only grants access if the submitted address exactly matches the single pre-approved administrator address (RULE-09); any other address is rejected silently, without confirming or denying whether it was recognised.

NFR-01

Performance

A fast, low-latency model is used specifically so the portal confirmation is not perceived as hung.

Performance

Security

Prevents forged payment-status events.

NFR-07

Availability

Scheduled automations shall be resilient to the hosting platform's plan-tier scheduling constraints by design.

The platform assumes daily-only execution rather than depending on higher frequency.

NFR-08

Scalability

The data model shall support a growing number of clients, requests and prospects without structural change.

Normalised relational schema with indexed foreign-key relationships (Section 15).

NFR-09

Accessibility

Interfaces shall use semantic, keyboard-navigable controls; status shall never be conveyed by colour alone.

Status is paired with an icon and text label in every case.

NFR-10

Reliability

Failure of a non-critical downstream integration (e.g. AI drafting) shall degrade gracefully.

A request can still be logged and reviewed manually even if AI drafting fails.

NFR-11

Maintainability

Shared business logic (e.g. invoice-overdue and lead-follow-up determination) shall be implemented once and reused everywhere it is displayed.

Prevents divergent behaviour between the admin console and the client portal.

NFR-12

Auditability

Every invoice, request and processed email shall retain a timestamped lifecycle record sufficient to reconstruct history.

Supports the Issues Log (Section 22) and general governance.

NFR-13

Compliance

Client communications shall be sent from a domain-verified sender address and retain a clear human contact path even where content is AI-drafted.

Preserves trust and deliverability (see ISS-03).

NFR-14

Compliance

Payment processing shall rely on a PCI-compliant third-party processor; the platform shall never store or transmit raw card data.

Card data never touches Hamish AI's own systems.

12. Use Cases

UC-01 — Submit and Triage a Client Request

Actors: Client; AI Triage Engine; Business Owner (Operator)

Trigger: A client submits a request through the portal, or emails Hamish AI directly.

Preconditions: The client has an active portal account, or is a recognised sender in the monitored inbox.

Main Flow

  • 1. Client enters free-text describing what they need (portal form) or sends an email.
  • 2. If received by email, the daily inbox job matches the sender to a client record and imports the message as a request.
  • 3. The AI Triage Engine reads the raw text and the client's maintenance-plan coverage.
  • 4. The engine assigns a category, complexity and priority, and determines whether the request is covered by maintenance.
  • 5. The engine determines whether the request is auto-sendable, needs clarification, or requires a task (see UC-02).
  • 6. The request record is saved with its full triage output for later reference.

Alternative Flows

  • Portal submission: the request is available for triage immediately, without waiting for the next scheduled inbox run.

Exception Flows

  • If the AI Triage Engine is unavailable, the request is still logged with the raw text, and the operator is left to triage it manually (NFR-10).
  • If an inbound email cannot be matched to any client, it is not imported as a request.

Post-conditions: A request record exists with a definite status (new, awaiting_info, or triaged) and, where applicable, a linked task.

UC-02 — Auto-Resolve or Flag a Request for Clarification

Actors: AI Triage Engine; Client; Business Owner (Operator)

Trigger: A request has just been triaged (continues from UC-01, step 5).

Preconditions: Triage output (category, complexity, priority, maintenance coverage) is available for the request.

Main Flow

  • 1. The engine evaluates whether the request meets the auto-send rule: high confidence AND covered by maintenance (RULE-01).
  • 2. If the rule is met, a client-facing reply is drafted and sent automatically; the request is marked auto_sent with a response timestamp.
  • 3. If the request is missing information needed to act, it is set to awaiting_info and specific clarifying questions are shown to the client.
  • 4. If neither applies, the request proceeds to task creation (UC not separately numbered — see Functional Requirement FR-03).

Alternative Flows

  • The client responds to an awaiting_info request with the missing detail; the request re-enters triage (effectively restarting UC-01 from step 3).

Exception Flows

  • If the auto-send email fails to deliver, the failure is logged and the request is not marked as responded to, so it remains visible for manual follow-up.

Post-conditions: The client has received either an automatic reply, a set of clarifying questions, or (via FR-03) a task has been created; in no case is the request left with no visible outcome.

UC-03 — Raise and Collect Payment for an Invoice

Actors: Business Owner (Operator); Client; Payment Processor

Trigger: The operator decides to bill a client for completed or agreed work.

Preconditions: The client has a verified email address on file (RULE-07).

Main Flow

  • 1. The operator enters an amount and description against the client's record.
  • 2. The platform creates (or reuses) a payment-processor customer record for the client.
  • 3. The platform creates and finalises an invoice with the payment processor, due in 14 days (RULE-06).
  • 4. The platform stores an invoice record locally, linked to the client (and, where applicable, the originating request).
  • 5. The platform emails the client a payment link, in the operator's own voice rather than the processor's default template.
  • 6. The client opens the link and pays via the processor's hosted payment page.
  • 7. The payment processor sends a signed webhook event confirming payment; the platform verifies the signature and marks the invoice paid, recording the payment timestamp.

Alternative Flows

  • The client does not pay before the due date: the invoice is treated as overdue (RULE-05) and appears on the Overview dashboard and the client detail page for a one-click reminder (UC not separately numbered — see FR-11).

Exception Flows

  • If invoice creation with the payment processor fails, no local invoice record is created and the operator is shown an explicit error rather than a false success.
  • If the webhook signature does not verify, the event is rejected and no invoice status is changed.

Post-conditions: A real, payable invoice exists with the payment processor and locally, its status is accurately reflected, and the client has been notified with a working payment link.

UC-04 — Research and Convert a Prospect to a Client

Actors: Scheduled Research Process; Business Owner (Operator); Prospect

Trigger: The weekly scheduled lead-research process runs.

Preconditions: None (this is the entry point of the acquisition pipeline).

Main Flow

  • 1. The scheduled process researches businesses in the Central Belt of Scotland matching defined categories.
  • 2. Candidate businesses are recorded as prospects with a status of needs_verification.
  • 3. The operator reviews and verifies candidates, moving qualified ones to ready.
  • 4. The operator requests an AI-drafted outreach email for a ready prospect; the engine matches the prospect's category to a relevant case study where one exists.
  • 5. The operator reviews and sends the drafted email, and marks the prospect contacted, which records the contact timestamp.
  • 6. If the prospect responds positively, the operator adds them as a client (Process 2 — Client Onboarding), completing the conversion.

Alternative Flows

  • No case-study match exists for the prospect's category: the outreach draft omits a proof point rather than forcing an irrelevant comparison.

Exception Flows

  • The prospect does not respond within 5 days: the lead is flagged needs follow-up (RULE-04) and reappears on the Overview dashboard until re-contacted or marked not a fit.

Post-conditions: The prospect is either converted to a client, marked not a fit, or remains visibly tracked for follow-up — no lead is silently forgotten.

UC-05 — Monitor a Client Website and Alert on an Issue

Actors: Scheduled Monitoring Process; Business Owner (Operator); Client (indirect)

Trigger: The daily scheduled site-check process runs.

Preconditions: The client has a website URL on file.

Main Flow

  • 1. The scheduled process checks each client website's uptime, SSL certificate validity, and for broken links.
  • 2. The raw results are stored as a site-check record with a timestamp.
  • 3. If any check is unhealthy, an alert email is sent to the operator the same day.
  • 4. If all checks are healthy, an AI-generated plain-English summary is stored for display in the client portal.

Alternative Flows

  • A client without a website URL on file is skipped entirely — this use case does not apply to them.

Exception Flows

  • If the target website itself is unreachable in a way that also prevents the check from completing, the failure is recorded rather than assumed healthy.

Post-conditions: The operator is aware of any website issue before the client typically would be, and the client portal reflects an accurate, current health summary.

UC-06 — Client Self-Service Portal Session

Actors: Client; Supabase Auth (identity provider)

Trigger: A client requests a sign-in link for the portal.

Preconditions: The client's email exactly matches the email on their client record (RULE-08 governs data scoping once signed in).

Main Flow

  • 1. The client enters their email on the portal login page and requests a link.
  • 2. A one-time magic link is emailed to that address.
  • 3. The client clicks the link; the platform exchanges it for an authenticated session.
  • 4. The platform matches the authenticated email to a client record.
  • 5. The client views their own request history, task status, website-health summary and billing status.
  • 6. The client may submit a new request or ask an instant question (UC-01 and FR-14 respectively) from within the same session.

Alternative Flows

  • The client asks an instant question via the knowledge base rather than submitting a full request.

Exception Flows

  • The authenticated email does not match any client record: the client sees an explicit no-access message rather than an error or another client's data.

Post-conditions: The client has a working session scoped strictly to their own data, per RULE-08.

13. Process Narratives

The following narratives walk through each of the seven processes exactly as modelled in the HamishAI Business Process Model, referencing the same node labels used in the diagram so the two artefacts can be read side by side.

13.1 Process 1 — Lead Generation

A weekly scheduled research task canvasses the Central Belt of Scotland for candidate businesses and writes them into the prospects table. Each prospect is checked for whether it has been verified and scored; unverified prospects return to the table for review, while verified, ready prospects proceed to an AI-drafted outreach email, personalised with a category-matched case study where one exists. The operator sends this email via Gmail. If the prospect has not replied within 5 days, the lead is flagged as needing follow-up and a further outreach draft is prepared; otherwise, once the prospect signs on, the flow continues into Process 2 (Client Onboarding).

13.2 Process 2 — Client Onboarding

The operator adds a client record, which immediately grants portal access — matched by email address, with no separate sign-up step required — and sets the client's maintenance plan (none, basic, or growth). From here, the client can submit requests (Process 3) and ask support questions (Process 4), and the maintenance plan determines how the client is billed (Process 6).

13.3 Process 3 — Request Handling & Fulfillment

A client submits a request via the portal, or emails Hamish AI directly, in which case the daily inbox cron picks it up. Either way, the request reaches AI triage, which determines category, complexity and priority. Triage then asks whether the request is both high-confidence and covered by the client's maintenance plan: if so, a reply is auto-sent to the client; if information is missing, the request is set to awaiting_info with explicit questions shown to the client, who can reply to re-enter the flow; if the request needs real work, a task is created, a calendar event is added with a due date set by priority, the operator completes the work, and the client is emailed that it is done.

13.4 Process 4 — Client Support

A client asks a question directly in the portal. The platform looks the question up against the curated knowledge base and returns an AI-drafted instant answer, without requiring operator involvement.

13.5 Process 5 — Site Monitoring

A daily site-check cron examines each client's website. If an issue is found — the site is down, its SSL certificate is invalid, or it has broken links — an alert is emailed to the operator. If the site is healthy, a plain-English summary is generated and shown in the client's portal.

13.6 Process 6 — Billing

The operator creates an invoice, which is created and finalised with the payment processor and emailed to the client as a payment link. The platform then asks whether the invoice is paid before its due date: if so, a webhook from the payment processor marks it paid, and its status becomes visible in the client's portal billing page; if the invoice becomes overdue, a reminder email is sent, returning the flow to the payment-link step until the invoice is settled.

13.7 Process 7 — Daily Overview

Four streams feed a single consolidated view for the operator: overdue invoices, requests stuck on awaiting_info, leads needing follow-up, and site issues. Each of these is itself the output of a decision point elsewhere in the model — the awaiting_info branch of Process 3, the overdue branch of Process 6, the follow-up flag of Process 1, and the issue-found branch of Process 5 — and each flows, dotted in the diagram to indicate a cross-cutting signal rather than a sequential step, into the /admin Overview dashboard.

16. Data Flow

Data enters the platform through three channels: direct client input (portal request and question submission), operator input (client onboarding, invoice creation, task progress), and scheduled automation (lead research, inbox ingestion, site checks). Every channel writes to the single Supabase-backed system of record described in Section 15; there is no secondary data store or spreadsheet in the target state.

From storage, data flows outward in two directions. First, into AI decision points (Section 18), which read request text, client coverage, prospect category and knowledge-base content to produce triage outcomes, drafts and answers that are written back to the same records. Second, into reporting surfaces: the client portal reads a client-scoped subset of the data (RULE-08); the admin console reads the full record set per client; and the Overview dashboard (Process 7) reads narrow, cross-client slices — overdue invoices, awaiting_info requests, stale leads, and unhealthy site checks — aggregating them into one view without duplicating the underlying data.

Outbound data leaves the platform as email (client replies, task-completion notices, invoice links, reminders, alerts, outreach) via the transactional email provider, and as payment instructions to the payment processor, which in turn reports back via webhook. No client data is transmitted to any party beyond what each integration in Section 17 requires to perform its specific function.

18. AI Decision Points

Five points in the model involve an AI-made determination that materially affects what a client sees or receives. Each is documented with its inputs, logic, outputs, confidence handling, and the human override that remains available in every case.

Decision 1 — Request Triage Classification

Aspect

Detail

Inputs

Raw request text; client profile (maintenance plan, package)

Logic

The AI model classifies the request into a category, complexity and priority, and determines whether it is covered by the client's maintenance plan

Outputs

category, complexity, priority, covered_by_maintenance, coverage_reasoning

Confidence Handling

There is no numeric confidence score exposed; instead, the auto-send gate (Decision 2) treats classification as usable only when the model itself signals a clear-cut case

Human Override

The draft_response field remains editable by the operator regardless of the auto-send outcome; every task remains fully operator-executed

Decision 2 — Auto-Send Eligibility

Aspect

Detail

Inputs

Output of Decision 1

Deliberately conservative — defaults to the awaiting_info or task-creation path whenever the criteria are not clearly met

Aspect

Detail

The lead's free-text category

Logic

Deliberately conservative — no match is forced for categories such as salons or generic retailers where no relevant case study exists

27. Benefits Realisation

27.1 Financial Benefits

  • Reduced incidence of late payment through automatic invoice reconciliation and one-click overdue reminders.
  • Reduced non-billable admin hours previously spent on manual triage, scheduling and payment chasing.
  • Reduced risk of lost new-business opportunity through automated lead follow-up flagging.

27.2 Operational Benefits

  • A single source of truth for every client, request, task, lead and invoice.
  • Reduced context-switching via the consolidated Overview dashboard (Process 7).
  • Consistent, auditable client communication regardless of the channel a client used.

27.3 Customer Benefits

  • Faster responses, including same-day resolution of routine, maintenance-covered requests.
  • Transparent, self-service visibility of request status, task progress, website health and billing.
  • Instant answers to common questions without waiting for a reply.
  • Earlier notice of website issues, ahead of the client discovering them independently.

27.4 Strategic Benefits

  • Hamish AI's own operation becomes a live reference case for the AI-automation services it sells to clients.
  • A scalable operating model supports growth in client volume without a proportional increase in operator time.
  • A documented, governed operating model (this pack) enables future onboarding of additional operators or investment discussions.

31. Change Impact Assessment

Dimension

Impact

People

Role consolidation is formalised (Section 4): one operator now explicitly holds Product Owner, Developer, Support, Account Management and Finance responsibilities. Clients gain a new self-service habit to adopt (the portal) in place of email/phone as their default contact channel.

Process

Replaces ad hoc, memory-dependent manual handling end to end with the seven-process model in Section 13. Every process now has a defined trigger, flow, and — where applicable — an automated or AI-assisted decision point.

Technology

Introduces dependency on six external SaaS providers (Section 17) and a single relational data platform as the system of record, where none previously existed.

Data

Establishes the business's first structured system of record. No historical pre-platform activity is migrated into it, with one exception: the pre-existing lead spreadsheet was migrated into the prospects table at go-live.

Governance

Establishes the first formal documentation pack and maintained process model for the business, together with an initial — deliberately lightweight, single-operator — RACI (Section 24) and RAID log (Section 23) where neither previously existed.

32. Recommendations

  • Progress the Google Cloud OAuth application beyond Testing status to remove the recurring 7-day reconnection burden (addresses RISK-03).
  • Formally promote the payment processor account from sandbox to live mode ahead of the first live client charge, following a documented go-live checklist (addresses RISK-04, ASM-04).
  • Consider recurring or subscription invoicing for maintenance-plan clients as a Phase 2 enhancement, building on the trust established by manual invoicing plus reminders (currently Won't, Section 25).
  • Consider a lightweight SLA or escalation tier on the Overview dashboard — for example, a distinct visual severity for items outstanding beyond a defined threshold — to close the residual process gap identified in Section 7.
  • Plan for a second-operator or role-based access model before client volume exceeds single-operator review capacity, to mitigate the bus-factor risk documented as RISK-02.
  • Periodically sample-audit auto-sent replies for accuracy as client and request volume grows, to catch any drift in AI triage behaviour early (supports NFR-01 and Decision 1/2 in Section 18).
  • Revisit the daily-only scheduling constraint (RISK-05) if near-real-time inbox ingestion becomes a business priority, by moving to a higher hosting tier.
